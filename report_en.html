1. Strategy Overview

Signal
Fires when either condition is met on the close: VIX close > 40 or S&P 500 weekly RSI(14) < 35.
Entry
After the signal, wait 9 trading days and buy TQQQ at the close of the 9th day.
Exit
Hold at least 1 year, then sell at the close of the first day the S&P 500 closes below its 100-day moving average (MA100). (The adopted version switches this to MA50 — see §8.)
Position
Full capital each trade, compounding; one position at a time; new signals ignored while invested.

6. Methodology & Assumptions

  • Instrument & data: traded instrument is TQQQ; the VIX and S&P 500 data for signals come from Yahoo Finance (yfinance, split/dividend-adjusted), updated to 2026-06-26.
  • Backtest window: bounded by the start of TQQQ and VIX data, 2010-02-11 → 2026-06-26.
  • No look-ahead: signals are computed on the daily close; the entry fills at the close 9 trading days later and the exit at the trigger-day close — strictly avoiding look-ahead bias.
  • RSI: S&P 500 weekly RSI(14), using a simple moving average of gains/losses (rolling(window=14).mean()), not Wilder's exponential smoothing.
  • Buy-and-hold benchmark: the simple formula $10,000 × (last close / first close) for un-traded TQQQ.
  • Metric definitions: Sharpe is daily-return annualized (×√252); max drawdown is on the daily equity peak; Alpha / Beta are against Buy & Hold TQQQ daily returns, annualized.

7. Real Trading: Advantages, Disadvantages & the Catch

The backtest numbers (10-for-10, ~43% CAGR, beating buy-and-hold) look spectacular — but it is precisely this "too perfect" quality that should make you wary. A sober, real-money assessment follows.

✅ Advantages

  • Buy in panic, sell in strength. Extreme VIX / deep oversold conditions have historically offered better forward risk/reward — systematically doing the most counter-human thing: being greedy when others are fearful.
  • In cash most of the time. Avoids the volatility decay of leveraged ETFs in choppy markets, taking 3× exposure only when a signal appears (roughly once every 1–2 years).
  • Rule-based and unemotional. The hardest part of "buying the dip" is actually pulling the trigger; a mechanical rule does it for you.
  • Better risk-adjusted than buy-and-hold TQQQ. Smaller max drawdown in the backtest (-62% vs -82%), higher Sharpe, positive alpha.
  • Cost- and tax-friendly. Very few trades; every hold exceeds 1 year, taxed as long-term capital gains, commissions negligible.

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Structural decay of a 3× leveraged ETF. TQQQ resets daily and decays in choppy markets even when QQQ is flat — it is a trading tool, not a buy-and-hold instrument.
  • Path dependence / risk of ruin. A single-day crash can be irreversible: QQQ -22% in a day (like 1987) ≈ TQQQ -66% in a day; a -34% underlying day is mathematically a wipeout. Leveraged ETFs can also be reverse-split or liquidated by the issuer.
  • The exit is loose and late. "Hold ≥ 1 year, then sell only when the S&P breaks MA100" can keep you in 3× exposure through much of a bear market (e.g. most of 2022). It is the most impactful yet most weakly defined piece.
  • All-in, single position, highly concentrated. No diversification; the whole bet rides on one instrument — a single point of failure.
  • Requires stomaching huge drawdowns. The strategy itself draws down over -60%. The backtest assumes you sit through it perfectly still — almost nobody actually can.
🎯 The biggest catch: "10 trades, 100% win rate" is a warning sign, not a selling point. Just 10 samples over 16 years proves nothing statistically — it is the textbook signature of overfitting to a benign regime — and we tuned parameters repeatedly on the same history (switching RSI from Wilder to SMA because "results were better," a 9-day delay that happened to buy lower). At its core this is a leveraged bet that "US tech always rebounds within 1–2 years": true since 2010 (zero rates, Fed backstop, an epic tech bull) but not a law of nature. In a 2000–2013 or Japan-1990-style "lost decade," "buy the dip + hold 3× leverage" compounds against you and the loose exit traps you. With leverage, one permanent loss of capital can wipe out ten prior wins. Also, the 43% CAGR is hugely amplified by TQQQ's tiny 2010 adjusted base ($0.21) and that once-in-a-generation bull — do not extrapolate.

🛡️ If you still trade it — risk-control advice

  • Don't go all-in at 3×. Size for the worst case; consider QQQ (1×) or QLD (2×) to cut ruin risk and decay.
  • Paper-trade / small-size a full cycle first for rolling out-of-sample validation, rather than trusting a single backtest.
  • Add a "catastrophe rule" (force de-leverage on an extreme single-day crash) — exactly where a backtest cannot protect you.
  • Diversify; never bet your whole net worth.
  • Pre-accept the drawdown. If you cannot stomach a -60% position, then however good the backtest, this strategy is untradable for you.
